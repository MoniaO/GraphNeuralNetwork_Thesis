\chapter{Related Work}
\label{ch:related-work}

This chapter reviews the background that motivates combining structured knowledge graphs with explicit statistical dependence estimates:
biomedical knowledge graphs, graph neural networks for heterogeneous relational data, Hierarchical Correlation Reconstruction, and knowledge-aware recommendation with evaluation reliability.

\section{Knowledge Graphs in Biomedicine}
\label{sec:related-kg}

Knowledge graphs represent structured knowledge through entities and the relations between them.
Formally, a knowledge graph $G$ may be viewed as a set of triples
\begin{equation}
G \subseteq \mathcal{V} \times \mathcal{R} \times \mathcal{V},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{V}$ denotes the set of entities (nodes) and $\mathcal{R}$ the set of relation types (edge labels).
Each triple $(h, r, t) \in G$ corresponds to a directed, labelled edge $h \xrightarrow{r} t$~\cite{hogan2021knowledge}.

The key benefit is an interlinked representation that is intelligible to both humans and machines~\cite{barrasa2023building}.
In biomedical applications, entities may represent drugs, diseases, genes, proteins, mechanisms, patient characteristics, or clinical outcomes, while edges describe typed relationships between them.
Such graphs are naturally heterogeneous, with node and edge type maps
\begin{equation}
G = (V, E, \tau, \phi),
\end{equation}
where $\tau: V \rightarrow \mathcal{T}_V$ and $\phi: E \rightarrow \mathcal{T}_E$~\cite{hu2020hgt}.

Knowledge graphs have been widely applied for integrating biomedical sources and supporting drug discovery~\cite{maclean2021knowledge}.
Many such graphs primarily encode associations from literature and curated databases rather than patient-specific empirical realisations of the mechanisms they describe, and often ``highlight biological associations, failing to model causal relationships in complex dynamic biological systems''~\cite{maclean2021knowledge}.
Recent work increasingly considers integrating knowledge graphs with causal or mechanistic representations~\cite{toonsi2025causal,lyu2023causal,mesinovic2025causal}.

\section{Graph Neural Networks for Relational and Heterogeneous Data}
\label{sec:related-gnn}

Graph neural networks (GNNs) learn node representations by aggregating information from neighbouring nodes through message passing~\cite{hamilton2017graphsage}.
After $K$ layers, a node's representation can incorporate its $K$-hop neighbourhood.
At layer $k$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:related-mp}
h_v^{(k)}
=
\mathrm{UPDATE}\!\Bigl(
h_v^{(k-1)},\
\mathrm{AGGREGATE}\bigl(\{h_u^{(k-1)} : u \in \mathcal{N}(v)\}\bigr)
\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Architectures differ mainly in how neighbours and, on heterogeneous graphs, relation types are transformed and aggregated.

GraphSAGE~\cite{hamilton2017graphsage} aggregates sampled neighbourhoods with mean, LSTM or pooling operators and concatenates the node's own representation, enabling inductive application to unseen nodes.
Relational Graph Convolutional Networks (R-GCNs)~\cite{schlichtkrull2018rgcn} apply relation-specific transformations,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:related-rgcn}
h_v^{(l+1)}
=
\sigma\!\left(
\sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}}
\sum_{u \in \mathcal{N}_r(v)}
\frac{1}{c_{v,r}}
W_r^{(l)} h_u^{(l)}
+
W_0^{(l)} h_v^{(l)}
\right),
\end{equation}
optionally with basis decomposition when the relation inventory is large.
Both families appear as structural baselines in Task~A.

Attention-based GNNs learn neighbour importance rather than using a fixed aggregation rule~\cite{velickovic2018gat}.
Transformer-style operators project node states into queries, keys and values and aggregate with softmax-normalised attention~\cite{vaswani2017attention}.
For heterogeneous graphs, the Heterogeneous Graph Transformer (HGT) makes attention scores and message transformations depend on source type, target type and edge relation~\cite{hu2020hgt}.
For an edge from source $s$ to target $t$, HGT represents semantics as the meta-relation $(\tau_s, \phi(e), \tau_t)$.
HGT is the primary structural encoder in Task~A and in the Last-FM* proxy, because typed pharmacotherapy and collaborative-knowledge-graph relations require type-aware message passing rather than a single shared convolution.

\section{Statistical Dependence and Hierarchical Correlation Reconstruction}
\label{sec:related-hcr}
\label{subsec:hcr_def}

Hierarchical Correlation Reconstruction (HCR), proposed by Jaros\l{}aw Duda, represents probability distributions and statistical dependencies through coefficients of an orthonormal basis expansion~\cite{duda2018hcr,duda2018hcr-ts,duda2020hcr-income,duda2023hcr-wolfram,duda2025hcr-mi}.
Lower-order coefficients describe broad dependence patterns; higher-order coefficients capture more complex departures from independence.

For variables $X$ and $Y$ with orthonormal bases $\{\phi_j^{(X)}\}$ and $\{\phi_k^{(Y)}\}$, empirical cross-dependence coefficients are
\begin{equation}
a_{jk}
=
\frac{1}{n}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\phi_j^{(X)}(X_i)\,
\phi_k^{(Y)}(Y_i).
\end{equation}
For a binary variable $B\in\{0,1\}$ with prevalence $p_B=P(B=1)$,
\begin{equation}
\phi_1(B)=\frac{B-p_B}{\sqrt{p_B(1-p_B)}},
\end{equation}
so the lowest-order coefficient $a_{11}$ is a standardized first-order dependence descriptor.
The same principle extends to other variable types via suitable marginal bases.

HCR is used here as a statistical representation of empirical dependence, not as a method for identifying causal effects.
Causal direction and mechanistic interpretation are supplied by the directed graph schema.
The term HCR denotes the orthonormal-basis coefficient construction; larger task-specific vectors that combine those coefficients with additional descriptors are called HCR-inspired representations.
In Task~A they describe the local dependency motif around a candidate edge; in Task~B they supply an endpoint-neighbourhood evidence channel alongside the GNN representation.
The common principle is combining learned graph representations with explicit statistical dependence estimated from observations.

\section{Knowledge-Aware Recommendation and Evaluation Reliability}
\label{sec:related-rec}

Beyond biomedicine, knowledge graphs are combined with empirical interaction data in recommendation systems.
KGAT propagates representations over a collaborative knowledge graph built from user--item interactions and external knowledge~\cite{wang2019kgat}.
The Last-FM benchmark from that line of work is used here as a cross-domain proxy for the link-prediction template developed on the pharmacotherapy graph.

The reliability of this benchmark was questioned by Shehzad et al.~\cite{shehzad2025lastfm} in a reproducibility study of intent-aware recommendation models.
The corrected Last-FM* split used in Chapter~\ref{ch:lastfm-proxy} removes train--test interaction leakage and is therefore the appropriate resource for methodological transfer claims.
