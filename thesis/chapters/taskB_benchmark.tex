\chapter{Cross-Domain Validation of Task B}
\label{ch:taskB-benchmark}
\label{subsec:benchmark-endpoint}

This chapter presents the cross-domain validation of Task B on two datasets:
Hetionet~\cite{hetionet} and {H2GB} {IEEE-CIS-G}~\cite{lin2025h2gb,h2gb}. 
There were two benchmarks run as 
Hetionet allows to assess models on similar graph structure as the synthetic task 
(directed graph with multiple target nodes), however it is not possible to 
assess it against external performance metrics as the graph was reconstructed. 
On the other hand, {H2GB} {IEEE-CIS-G} Dataset is one label node classification task
without directed links between nodes, but it allows to assess models against external 
performance metrics. Taken together, the two settings test whether extending the GNN with a 
statistical evidence branch remains useful for node classification under different 
graph structures and domains.

\section{Hetionet}
\label{sec:hetionet}

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Proxy Construction}
\label{sec:hetionet-problem}

Task~B is evaluated on a benchmark dataset derived from Hetionet~\cite{hetionet}. 
Hetionet is a heterogeneous network
(\emph{hetnet}) that integrates biomedical knowledge from 29 publicly
available resources. It represents biological and pharmacological entities
as nodes and documented relationships between them as typed edges.

Hetionet v1.0 contains 47{,}031 nodes belonging to 11 node types and
2{,}250{,}197 relationships of 24 types~\cite{himmelstein2017hetionet}. The graph includes compounds,
diseases, genes, anatomies, pathways, biological processes, molecular
functions, cellular components, pharmacologic classes, side effects, and
symptoms. This heterogeneous structure makes Hetionet suitable for node classification task described further. 
The proxy construction and experimental protocol are specified in the following subsections.

\subsection{Data Representation}
\label{sec:hetionet-data}

The
benchmark on this dataset is a structural proxy---the
pipeline, layer slots, and statistical evidence branch are reused, but the graph is
reconstructed to resemble the synthetic schema in topology. 
The reconstruction proceeds as follows. 

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{Instance.} A compound takes the role of the patient: the
    topology is shared and a compound is described by the edges linking it to
    the remaining entities. A compound is therefore \emph{not} a node of the
    graph, which means an entity can serve as a feature column only if it
    connects to compounds directly.

  \item \textbf{Target.} The predicted endpoint is a disease node, labelled positive when a
  \texttt{treats} or \texttt{palliates} edge links the compound to that disease
  (the two relations were merged).
  Prevalences range from $1.29\%$ to $4.51\%$ over the twenty most frequent
  diseases. 

  \item \textbf{Layer mapping.} Node types were mapped onto the slots of the
    synthetic schema as presented in \cref{tab:hetionet-mapping}.

  \item \textbf{Direction.} Hetionet edges are undirected associations.
    For pipeline compatibility, every retained edge was oriented from the
    lower to the higher synthetic layer and intra-layer edges were
    discarded, guaranteeing acyclicity. 

  \item \textbf{Path length.} Two changes lengthen the paths to a disease. First, a pathway and
  biological-process layer was inserted as the third layer, while the direct
  \texttt{DaG} edges were kept. Second, class-to-gene edges were added, because
  pharmacologic classes connect only to compounds in Hetionet and would otherwise
  have no edges at all. Such an edge is created when at least three training
  compounds of a class bind the same gene.

\end{enumerate}

The proxy cut comprises $1{,}552$ compounds and twenty disease endpoints.
Model selection follows the same validation-driven protocol as on the synthetic Task~B cohort.

\label{sec:hetionet-results}

% The models on benchmark dataset shows the same trend as on synthethic dataset. 
% The approach with HCR significantly improves results comparing to baseline model without HCR. 
% It generates similar results as models with residual connections, but what it is interesting on the benchamrk dataset
% HCR combined with residual connections reaches the higher results than separated approach. 

The benchmark reproduces several trends
observed on the synthetic dataset -- depth sensitivity without stabilisers,
partial recovery with the statistical evidence branch -- under a different graph semantics 
and without
oracle metrics.
Reported scores in \cref{tab:proxy-full} are the mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation
over up to three seeds.
The gain from statistical evidence is conditional on the state of the
propagation path: at $L=6$ without residual connections the graph-only model
reaches $0.615 \pm 0.026$ test AUROC, while statistical-evidence variants recover
part of the lost ranking quality ($0.703$--$0.712$).
At $L=1$, where the receptive field of an endpoint coincides with the input of
the statistical evidence branch, graph-only and statistical-evidence variants remain close
(test AUROC $0.667$--$0.672$).

At $L=6$, residual connections already restore much of the graph pathway
($0.728 \pm 0.010$ without the statistical branch).
Adding statistical evidence on top of residuals yields $0.726$--$0.742$, so the
two mechanisms are only weakly additive on this proxy: the residual path carries
most of the depth-related gain, while the statistical branch helps mainly when
that path is absent.

The reason is the structure of the two graphs. In the synthetic graph the direct
parents of an endpoint are observed, so the statistical evidence branch and the one-hop
receptive field read the same variables and the two mechanisms address the same
bottleneck. In the Hetionet-derived graph depth is more crucial because some
layers are latent. Information must
therefore traverse several hops, and the residual path and the HCR bypass
contribute at different points of that route.

Overall, the proxy experiment suggests that combining message passing
with the statistical evidence branch remains useful with 
multi-hop reasoning. Whether an
additional residual path helps or hinders depends on where 
the statistical
evidence enters the model.

\section{H2GB IEEE-CIS-G}
\label{sec:ieee-cis}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:ieee-problem}

The H2GB IEEE-CIS-G dataset is one of the graph datasets assembled for the H2GB project~\cite{lin2025h2gb,h2gb}, whose purpose is to study heterogeneity and heterophily in graph structure and modelling.
The project comprises nine datasets across five domains (academia, e-commerce, finance, cybersecurity, and social science) and benchmarks 28 baseline models under both properties simultaneously.

IEEE-CIS-G originates from the IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection Kaggle
competition~\cite{howard2019ieeecis}, based on verified transaction data from Vesta Corporation, a
payment-services company.
The original release is tabular: the labelled training table contains $590{,}540$ card transactions, of which $20{,}663$ ($3.50\%$) are fraudulent, and an accompanying identity table covers $144{,}233$ of these transactions ($24.4\%$).
The prediction task is binary node classification: whether a transaction is fraudulent.

Lin et al.~\cite{lin2025h2gb} restructure the labelled records that have identity information as 
a heterogeneous bipartite graph.
IEEE-CIS-G comprises $153{,}880$ nodes of $12$ types and $2{,}873{,}472$ edges of $22$ relation types: 
each transaction node is linked to eleven metadata entity types (card information, product code, 
purchaser and recipient email domain, and related attributes).
Transaction nodes carry a $4823$-dimensional feature vector and a binary fraud label.
Restricting to the identity-linked labelled subset yields $144{,}233$ transaction nodes, of 
which $11{,}318$ ($7.85\%$) are fraudulent, matching the $12{:}1$ class imbalance in 
Table~1 of~\cite{lin2025h2gb}.
The $\approx 4\%$ positive rate quoted in the H2GB dataset description is the original 
Kaggle training table ($3.50\%$), before this identity filter.

\subsection{Data Representation}
\label{sec:ieee-data}

Unlike the Hetionet proxy, IEEE-CIS-G is not reconstructed onto the synthetic DAG.
The Task~B pipeline and statistical evidence branch are reused, but node-type
roles are mapped onto the released H2GB bipartite graph.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \textbf{Instance.} A transaction takes the role of the patient.
    Unlike the Hetionet compound, the transaction \emph{is} a graph node and
    carries the $4823$-dimensional feature vector.

  \item \textbf{Target.} The predicted endpoint is the binary fraud label
    \texttt{isFraud} on the transaction node ($7.85\%$ positive), not a
    separate endpoint node type.

  \item \textbf{Layer mapping.} Node types and features are mapped onto the
    slots of the synthetic schema as presented in \cref{tab:ieee-cis-mapping}.
    Metadata entity types occupy the observed context and exposure slots;
    Vesta and identity fields remain node features on the transaction;
    the fraud mechanism has no node type and is treated as latent.

  \item \textbf{Direction.} IEEE-CIS-G is an undirected bipartite star already
    stored as $22$ directed relation types (\texttt{to} and \texttt{rev\_to}).
    Edges are left in this H2GB orientation; they are not re-cut into an
    acyclic layer chain.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Training and Evaluation}
\label{sec:ieee-protocol}

H2GB evaluates a suite of graph architectures on IEEE-CIS-G, including
GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT, HGT, R-GCN, SHGN and $\mathrm{H}^{2}$G-former, under a
common protocol whose focus is heterophily in heterogeneous graphs~\cite{lin2025h2gb}.
The present experiment does not re-run that models set.
The aim is to repeat the Task~B \emph{deep and wide} design on the same IEEE-CIS-G
input: a TransformerConv encoder, as on the synthetic cohort, enriched with the
HCR-inspired statistical evidence branch.

As the graph is a bipartite star, evidence is
attached at the parent--endpoint level only.
The endpoint is \texttt{isFraud} on the transaction; parents are tabular Vesta
and identity columns that are not already graph node types
(\texttt{V*}, \texttt{C*}, \texttt{D*}, amount, and low-cardinality
\texttt{M*}/device dummies).
Grandparent--parent--endpoint \emph{triple} statistics from the synthetic
pipeline are omitted: IEEE-CIS-G has no typed three-hop causal chain, and
materialising triples over shared metadata nodes would explode the evidence
tensor.

Pairwise evidence uses the $42$-channel layout of Task~B
(\cref{subsec:hcr_application}): matrix-energy summaries, marginals, type
indicators and the patient activation channel, with leave-one-out coefficients
on the training split.
On the training transactions, $408$ candidate parents pass support and variance
filters; the $64$ strongest by a unified association rank
($\varphi$-coefficient for binary columns, point-biserial for continuous)
form the parent vocabulary.
Each transaction is then packed into $K=16$ slots, keeping the highest-ranked
active parents.
A shared pair encoder (two-layer MLP, $42 \rightarrow 16 \rightarrow 4$)
embeds every slot, mean-pools over the mask, and adds a learned scalar to the
fraud logit of the TransformerConv head.

During training sampling of neighbourhood follows the H2GB loader -- \texttt{HGTLoader} with a
per-type neighbourhood budget, class weights $1{:}5$, and L1
\texttt{NormalizeFeatures}.
The labelled graph is split temporally $80/10/10$.
The checkpoint is selected on validation binary F1 as it was metric chosen by authors. 
Test F1 uses threshold $0.5$.
Depth $L\in\{2,3,6\}$ is crossed with residual connections and with or without
the statistical branch, over seeds $42$, $20256$ and $202567$.

\subsection{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:ieee-results}

Reported scores in \cref{tab:ieee-cis-full} are the mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation
over three seeds ($42$, $20256$, $202567$).
The checkpoint is selected on validation binary F1; test metrics are taken from that checkpoint,
and F1 uses a decision threshold of $0.5$.
Without residual connections the graph-only model is unstable: test F1 is
$0.018 \pm 0.031$ at $L=2$ and $0.008 \pm 0.014$ at $L=6$.
Residual connections restore a stable graph pathway
($0.299$--$0.306$ test F1; $0.761$--$0.764$ test AUROC).
Adding the statistical evidence branch on top of residuals raises the mean test F1 to
$0.450$--$0.454$, but the seed spread is large
(\cref{fig:ieee-cis-ablation}): two seeds sit near $0.50$, while seed $20256$ remains close to the residual-only band.

\Cref{fig:ieee-cis-h2gb} places the $L=3$ HCR model next to the IEEE-CIS-G F1 numbers published
 with H2GB~\cite{lin2025h2gb}.
The comparison is on the same IEEE-CIS-G input: an HGT reimplementation matching the 
H2GB IEEE-CIS-HGT configuration recovered the published HGT F1, 
which confirms that the graph, splits, sampler and metric agree with the benchmark.
