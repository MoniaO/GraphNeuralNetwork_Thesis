\subsubsection{Architecture}

The first aim of experiments was to choose the best architecture for the problem. The following architecture was 
tested RGCN, SAGE, Transformer. 
 
The results for these architectures are reported in \cref{tab:arch-nores}.
The following conventions apply to this and all subsequent result tables.

\emph{Lift} is the AUPRC divided by the prevalence baseline, that is, the
factor by which the model improves over a random ranking; at a mean
prevalence of $5.5\%$ a lift of $2.3$ corresponds to an AUPRC of $0.125$.

\emph{\%~ceiling} is defined as
%
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{AUC} - 0.5}{\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{Bayes}} - 0.5} \cdot 100\%,
  \label{eq:pct-ceiling}
\end{equation}
%
where $\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{Bayes}} = 0.651$ is the Bayes ceiling averaged
over the same eight endpoints. It expresses the
share of attainable signal recovered by the model. Both terms are subtracted
by $0.5$ so that a random classifier scores $0\%$.
Unless stated otherwise, the epoch is selected on the validation set and
test metrics are reported from that epoch.
%R-GCN with residual connections was only run with mean aggregation and is therefore absent from the lower block.
In \cref{tab:arch-nores} there are included only layers 1,3 and 6 to show the trend with the increased number of layers.

Without residual connections the three architectures diverge sharply at
L=6: GraphSAGE retains 82\% of the ceiling on the test set, while R-GCN
and TransformerConv collapse to constant predictions (AUC=0.50). 
%Since the root transform was disabled in all three, this cannot be attributed to an implicit skip connection. 
The difference lies in how the neighbourhood term is normalised — mean aggregation in GraphSAGE against learned 
softmax weights in TransformerConv and degree normalisation in R-GCN.

    With residual connections all three architectures converge to comparable
    performance (\cref{tab:arch-res}): 69--80\% of the Bayes ceiling on the test
    set, with differences of about 0.01 AUC. The choice of architecture for the remaining experiments was
    therefore made on structural grounds. TransformerConv
    is the only one of the three that conditions message passing on edge
    attributes, which the audited DAG provides (mechanistic confidence, evidence
    weight, effect size and sign). Since the
    role of edge-level evidence is important for casual classification,
    TransformerConv was selected for all subsequent experiments with HCR.
